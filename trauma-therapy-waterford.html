Trauma & PTSD Therapy in Waterford

Paced, careful work — you set what is opened and when

Trauma therapy is careful, paced work on experiences that have left a lasting mark on how you feel, react and relate. You decide what is opened and when. I offer trauma and PTSD therapy in Waterford, and online or by telephone, from a Gestalt and integrative perspective.

How trauma shows up later

Trauma is less about the event and more about what it left behind. It can appear as being permanently on alert, as flashbacks or intrusive memories, as sleep that does not restore, or as a numbness that keeps everything at a distance.

It often shows up in relationships: difficulty trusting, a startle response that others do not understand, a tendency to leave before you can be left. Many people do not connect these to anything in particular, or assume that what happened to them was not serious enough to count.

Trauma can follow a single event — an accident, an assault, a sudden loss. It can also build over years, which is sometimes described as complex trauma or CPTSD, where the harm came from ongoing circumstances or relationships rather than one incident.

Safety and pace come first

Effective trauma work does not begin with the trauma. It begins with stability: understanding your own responses, building resources you can rely on, and establishing that this room is somewhere you have control.

You are never required to describe what happened in detail, and you will not be pushed to revisit anything before you are ready. Retelling a traumatic experience without sufficient stability can do harm, and avoiding that is part of the work, not a delay to it.

Gestalt therapy's attention to the present moment is useful here. We can work with how the past lives in you now — in your body, your reactions, your relationships — without requiring you to go back into it.

What change can look like

The aim is not to erase what happened. It is to reduce its grip: fewer intrusions, more room between a trigger and your response, and a growing sense that you are living in the present rather than being pulled back.

This work often takes longer than other therapy, and it does not move in a straight line. We review together, and you remain in control of the pace throughout.

Where other support may be needed

Some presentations of trauma need specialist or multi-disciplinary care, and I will say so plainly if I think that is the case rather than continue work that is not the right fit. I can suggest where to look.

Counselling is not an emergency service. If you are in immediate distress, contact your GP or your nearest Emergency Department, or call Samaritans on 116 123 or Pieta House on 1800 247 247. In an emergency, call 999 or 112.
