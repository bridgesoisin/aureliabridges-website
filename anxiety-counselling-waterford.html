Anxiety Counselling in Waterford

Understanding what anxiety is protecting you from, and finding a steadier way to live

Anxiety counselling helps you understand what your anxiety is responding to, recognise the patterns that keep it going, and build a steadier relationship with your own thoughts and body. I offer anxiety counselling in Waterford, and online or by telephone, using a Gestalt and integrative approach.

What anxiety actually feels like

Anxiety is rarely just worrying. It can be a tight chest on the drive to work, a mind that will not settle at two in the morning, a stomach that turns before a conversation you have been dreading for weeks. It can look like being fine — capable, organised, getting everything done — while running on adrenaline underneath.

Some people describe a constant low hum they have lived with so long it feels like personality rather than distress. Others describe panic that arrives without warning and leaves them frightened of the next one. Both are worth bringing to therapy. You do not need to be in crisis, and you do not need to be able to explain why you feel this way.

How Gestalt therapy works with anxiety

Gestalt therapy pays close attention to what is happening in the present moment — including in your body. That matters with anxiety, because anxiety lives in the body as much as the mind. Noticing where you hold it, and what happens just before it rises, is often more useful than arguing with the thoughts.

Rather than treating anxiety as a fault to be removed, we get curious about what it is doing. Anxiety is frequently a form of protection: it anticipates threat, it prepares, it keeps you scanning. Understanding what it is protecting you from often loosens its grip more than trying to suppress it.

From there we work on what helps: recognising early signals, interrupting the spiral, and building tolerance for the feelings underneath — which are often sadness, anger or need rather than fear itself.

What you can expect

We begin where you are. In a first session you can share as much or as little as feels manageable, and I will explain how I work and answer any questions. There is no assessment to pass and no pressure to produce a tidy account of yourself.

Some people come for a short piece of work around a specific difficulty — a presentation, a health worry, a period of acute stress. Others stay longer to understand a pattern that has been with them for years. We review together, and you are free to end therapy whenever you choose.

When anxiety needs more than counselling

Counselling is not an emergency service. If your anxiety comes with thoughts of harming yourself, or you feel unable to keep yourself safe, please contact your GP, or Samaritans on 116 123, or Pieta House on 1800 247 247. In an emergency, call 999 or 112.

I do not prescribe medication. If medication might help, I may suggest you speak with your GP — therapy and medication often work well together.
