Depression Counselling in Waterford

For low mood, exhaustion, and the sense that something has gone flat

Counselling for depression offers a space to say what is actually happening, to understand what has been unexpressed or unmet, and to begin recovering a sense of your own agency. I offer depression counselling in Waterford, and online or by telephone, working from a Gestalt and integrative perspective.

Depression is not always sadness

People often expect depression to feel like crying. Frequently it feels like nothing at all — a flatness, a greyness, a sense of watching your own life from slightly outside it. Things that used to matter stop mattering. Getting up takes a negotiation.

It can also arrive as irritability, exhaustion that sleep does not fix, or a harsh internal voice that narrates everything you do badly. Many people who are depressed continue to function well outwardly, which can make it harder to name and harder to be taken seriously.

How therapy helps

Gestalt therapy approaches low mood by looking at what is being held down. Depression often involves feelings that have had nowhere to go — grief that was never mourned, anger that was never safe to express, needs that were never met or even acknowledged.

Rather than trying to talk you out of how you feel, we work on making room for it, and on noticing what happens in you as you speak. Sometimes what looks like emptiness turns out to be a great deal held very tightly.

We also pay attention to the practical shape of your life: what sustains you, what depletes you, and what small changes are actually available to you right now — not as a checklist, but as something to explore at your own pace.

Working at a pace that is manageable

When you are depressed, even coming to therapy can feel like an effort. I will not push you to disclose more than you want to, and there is no expectation that you arrive with insight. Some sessions are about very little. That is allowed.

If you are also seeing your GP, or taking medication, therapy sits alongside that rather than replacing it. With your permission I am happy to liaise with your GP.

If you are having thoughts of suicide

Counselling is not an emergency service. If you are thinking about ending your life, or feel unable to keep yourself safe, please contact your GP or your nearest Emergency Department, or call Pieta House on 1800 247 247 or Samaritans on 116 123, both free and available 24 hours. In an emergency, call 999 or 112.

These feelings are common in depression and they are treatable. Reaching out is not an overreaction.
